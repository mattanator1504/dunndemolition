## Dunn's own positions (from the live site)

- **Not all demolition contractors are the same.** Professional practices vary over a wide range: skilled people, the right equipment and proper procedures are the difference.
- **Check for actual demolition insurance.** Ask for the declaration page of the policy, which states what they're insured to do. Clients are often surprised how many demolition jobs are done by landscapers instead of professional demolition companies.
- **No explosives.** We demolish with excavators and hydraulic thumbs, for control and safety.
- **Recycle whenever possible.** 50–75% of removed material gets recycled. Non-recyclable debris goes to environmentally approved landfills.
- **Don't spread a crew too thin.** We never run more than 3 projects at a time, so every job gets managed properly.
- **Keep the site clean as you go.** Debris is loaded and hauled off continuously during demolition, not left in a pile until the end.
- **Permits shouldn't be the client's headache.** We help pull demolition permits and handle the disconnect and municipal steps.

## Universal (safe for any job)

- **Get the price in writing before work starts.** If the job turns out bigger than expected, stop, re-quote and wait for a yes.
- **"Starting at" pricing is a red flag.** A demolition contractor should see the site and quote the job.
- **The cheapest quote is rarely the cheapest outcome.** Back this with a real example once Dunn gives us one (`stories.md`).
- **Make sure the quote covers hauling and disposal,** not just knocking it down.
- **Clean up.** Sounds obvious. Apparently isn't.
- **Don't badmouth the last contractor,** even if they earned it.
- **Tell people when they don't need us.** If a shed can be taken apart by hand over a weekend, say so first. This is the single biggest voice tell.

## What customers should do first

These must stay safe and accurate.

- **Find out whether you need a permit.** Most cities require a demolition permit for whole structures. We can help, but knowing early saves weeks.
- **Get the utilities disconnected.** Gas, electric, water and sewer have to be disconnected before a building comes down. Call the providers early, because this is often the slowest step.
- **Ask every contractor for their insurance declaration page.** It takes them a minute to send. If they can't, that tells you something.
- **Look for buried tanks and old septic systems.** An oil tank or septic tank found mid-job changes the plan and the price.
- **Safety line:** don't DIY structural demolition, anything near live utilities, or anything that might contain asbestos or lead. Get it assessed first. [CONFIRM with Dunn how they handle asbestos surveys before publishing anything specific.]
