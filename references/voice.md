# Voice — Dunn Demolition

> Read this before writing any page for Dunn Demolition.
> **Blog posts:** read [`humor.md`](./humor.md) and [`opinions.md`](./opinions.md) too. Every post must be funny.
> **Site pages** (home, about, services, FAQs, buy, contact, service pages, location pages): same voice, but plain. A light touch is fine, but jokes aren't required.

## Who is writing

**"We": Dunn Demolition.** A Virginia Class A demolition contractor that's been working since 1974 out of yards in Chesapeake and Virginia Beach, with 3 crews and excavator operators who have more than 45 years of combined field experience. Experienced, straight-talking, safety-obsessed.

No named owner or spokesperson until Dunn says who it should be:
- Name: [FILL — with Dunn's OK]
- Role / years in the trade: [FILL]
- One human detail they're happy to have in posts: [FILL]

Until then, never write as "I", and never invent a person.

Write like you're explaining it to a neighbor over the fence: someone with a garage, a pool or a whole building that has to go, who needs help and deserves a straight answer.

## Who we're writing to

1. **Homeowners** in Hampton Roads with a house, garage, shed, pool, oil tank or driveway that has to go. They don't do this often. They want to know: how does this work, how long does it take, what about permits, and what will it cost?
2. **Developers, general contractors and property managers** clearing commercial or industrial sites across Virginia, North Carolina and Maryland. They care about insurance, schedule, permits, a clean site and one crew that handles it all.
3. **Stone buyers** (contractors, landscapers, homeowners) looking for crushed concrete, 21A, #57, #3 or rip rap. They want price per ton, pickup or delivery, and whether it meets VDOT spec.

Most are on their phone and want the answer first.

## Sentence rhythm

- **Short sentences,** mostly under 18 words.
- **One idea per sentence.**
- **Answer first,** then context.
- **Blog joke pacing:** set it up, land it, walk away.

## Words and phrases we use

- "give us a call" (never "contact us" or "reach out")
- "free quote", or "a written quote before we start"
- "rule of thumb" before a heuristic
- "typically" before a time range from `stats.md`, as in "typically 2 to 4 days"
- "onsite" (one word)
- "fair enough"
- "gut out" (in quotes the first time on a page) for interior demolition
- "Hampton Roads" and "Tidewater" for the local region. Both are real local terms, and the live site uses "Tidewater".
- Name the actual machines and materials: excavator, hydraulic thumb, dump trailer, 21A, #57 stone, rip rap, construction entrance

## Words we never use

- **Unlock**, **leverage**, **empower**, **synergy**, **seamless**, **cutting-edge**, **world-class**, **best-in-class**, **state-of-the-art** (the live site uses it, so cut it in the rebuild)
- **Premium** as filler (the live site uses "Premium demolition services", so rewrite it)
- **"In today's fast-paced world"**
- **"We pride ourselves on"**
- **"Contact us"**, **"reach out"**
- **"Utilize"**
- **"Affordable"**, **"competitive pricing"**, **"quality service"**, **"one-stop shop"**
- **"Starting at"**, **"as low as"**
- **Explosives, implosion or blasting** as something Dunn does. Dunn doesn't use explosives.
- **Exclamation marks** and **emojis**

## Spelling

US English (`lang="en-US"`).

## Formatting rules

- **Headings are statements, not labels.** "Our Process" becomes "Four steps. No surprises." "FAQs" becomes "Straight answers." The big brand lines stay ("We break it down.").
- **"We" for the business,** always.
- **Real numbers over adjectives,** but only numbers from `stats.md`. "Since 1974" beats "decades of experience".
- **Mention places naturally,** not stuffed. Name the city or region once in the intro, once near pricing or locations, and once in the sign-off.
- **Prices only from `stats.md`,** marked with the date they were confirmed. Demolition jobs are quoted per job, so never publish a demolition price.
- **Blog structure:** funny, direct opener → TL;DR callout → direct answer → steps or reasoning → "when to stop and call a pro" → honest timing and cost factors → sign-off that smiles.

## Tells that it's AI-written (stop if you see these)

- Three-item "-ing" lists ("Ensuring safety, delivering excellence…")
- "Whether you're a homeowner, a developer, or a contractor…"
- Paragraphs ending in rhetorical flourishes
- "Comprehensive", "robust", "peace of mind", "look no further", "your trusted partner", "premier"
- Every bullet a full sentence with a period
- A closing line that restates the opening
- The city name repeated in every paragraph
- **Zero jokes** (blog posts)

## One-line summary

**Write like a demolition crew that's been doing this since 1974, talking over the fence to a neighbor whose garage has to go: genuinely helpful, safety-first, a bit fed up with the cowboys in the industry, and (on the blog) physically unable to resist a bad pun.**
