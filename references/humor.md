# Humor — Dunn Demolition

> **Blog posts only.** Don't use this on the home, about, services, FAQs, buy, contact, service or location pages. Those stay plain (see `voice.md`).
> Demolition paperwork is boring. Your job is to make it fun. Every blog post is funny, and this isn't optional.
> Read this file **before** writing a single sentence of a post.

---

## The north star

The voice is a demolition crew that's been knocking things down since 1974 and has seen everything twice. The humor sits between two characters:

- **Ted Lasso:** how we treat the reader. Kind. Genuinely on their side. Folksy metaphors that shouldn't work and somehow do. Someone whose house is about to be torn down, or whose contractor just vanished, gets reassurance first and jokes second.
- **Larry David:** how we treat the industry. Petty, specific outrage at the small rules everyone breaks. The "demolition contractor" who's really a landscaper with a rented excavator. The quote that forgot about disposal. Mud tracked halfway down the street.

Warm to the person. Mildly unhinged about the nonsense around them.

There's no named person at Dunn to joke about yet (see `voice.md`). Until Dunn says otherwise, punch at the industry, at paperwork and at physics, never at a made-up person.

## The opening rule

If the first 50 words don't land a topic pun, a specific grievance or a bracketed aside, rewrite them. We are not a brochure. We are the demolition guy at the barbecue who just got asked "quick question about my shed" and is secretly delighted.

## Six rules for humor in every post

### 1. One topic pun is mandatory.

Every post needs at least one pun or metaphor built from the actual topic:

- Pool removal: "Before you take the plunge, here's what filling in a pool really involves."
- Oil tank removal: "Nobody wants their home sale to get tanked by a buried oil tank."
- Interior gut-outs: "Trust your gut. Then call someone with a dumpster."
- Recycled concrete: "Let's talk concrete numbers."
- Permits: "We'll break it to you gently: the permit takes longer than the demolition."

Lay it down, walk away, get back to helping.

### 2. Ted energy: root for the reader.

They're here because something big is about to happen to their property. Reassure them first.

- "Tearing down a house sounds enormous. A typical residential demolition takes 2 to 4 days. The paperwork beforehand is the longer part, and we help with that."
- "You're not overthinking it. Asking a demo contractor for their insurance declaration page is exactly the right move."

Optimism has to be specific. "Don't worry" is a fridge magnet. "Clean concrete is free to drop off at our yard" is Ted. Use only numbers from `stats.md`.

### 3. Larry energy: get irrationally specific about industry nonsense.

Target behaviors only. Never a named business, and never the customer.

Good targets:
- The "demolition contractor" who's a landscaper with a rented excavator and no demolition insurance.
- A quote that covers knocking it down and quietly forgets taking it away.
- "We'll start next week," every week, for a month.
- Mud and debris tracked down the street because nobody put in a construction entrance.
- Rebar left sticking out of the dirt as a "souvenir".
- "We don't pull permits. That's on you."

The format is to state it like a social rule everyone should already know, and hold the line:

> "If you tear something down, you take it away. I didn't invent this rule. I'm just enforcing it."

(That's the only "I" allowed, as a rhetorical aside. The voice is still "we".)

### 4. Brackets are your best friend.

- "(Yes, we really do remove trees. People are always surprised.)"
- "[Takes a deep breath, then reads the municipal disconnect checklist out loud.]"
- "(If you're still reading this far into a post about rip rap classes, you're our kind of person.)"

### 5. Pop culture that ages well.

The main sources are **Ted Lasso** and **Curb Your Enthusiasm**. Also safe: **Seinfeld, The Office, Jaws, Die Hard, Shawshank, classic Simpsons, Parks and Rec, Home Improvement, This Old House.**

- **Allude, don't quote.** One catchphrase-level nod per post at most. Never lift full lines.
- **The reference must explain something.** If cutting it loses nothing, cut it.
- **No current memes.**

Safe landings:
- "Finding an old oil tank under the yard is the 'we're gonna need a bigger boat' moment of a home sale."
- "Tim Taylor would want more power. We just want the utilities disconnected first."

### 6. The sign-off has to land.

Not: "Contact Dunn Demolition for all your demolition needs."

But: "If it's got to come down, give us a call. We'll knock it down, haul it off and recycle most of it. We'll also, probably, make a terrible pun. Consider that a bonus, not a warning."

## Anti-patterns: never do these

- **Exclamation marks.** Deadpan lands better.
- **Emojis,** anywhere.
- **Laughing at the customer,** ever.
- **Naming competitors.** Behaviors, not businesses.
- **Inventing a person to joke about.** No made-up crew member, foreman or family until Dunn gives us a real one.
- **Joking about injuries, collapses or safety.** Safety is the one thing that's never the punchline.
- **Explosion or implosion jokes that suggest we blow things up.** We don't use explosives.
- **Edgy.** It's not the brand.
- **The same joke twice in one post.**
- **Meta jokes about the post,** once at most.

## Frequency

**One moment every 300–500 words.** A 1,500-word post lands roughly:

- **2–3** topic puns
- **1–2** Larry grievances
- **1–2** Ted reassurance lines
- **1–2** bracketed asides
- **1** sign-off that smiles

## Example openings: copy the energy, not the jokes

> Every pool removal starts with the same sentence: "We barely use it." Fair enough. Here's what it takes to make a pool disappear, and why it's not as simple as filling it with dirt.

> Somewhere in Hampton Roads right now, a landscaper is telling a homeowner he "does demo too." (He has a rented excavator and a can-do attitude. He does not have demolition insurance.) Here's how to check before you sign anything.

> Short answer: a typical house demolition takes 2 to 4 days. Long answer: the house is the easy part. The permit, the utility disconnects and that oil tank nobody mentioned take longer, so let's break it down.
